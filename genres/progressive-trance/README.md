# Progressive Trance

## Genre Overview

Progressive trance is the patient branch of trance: slower, deeper and built on gradual change instead of one big moment. It grew out of British progressive house in the early-to-mid 1990s, when DJs and producers stretched trance's melodic language over long, evolving arrangements. Labels such as Hooj Choons, Platipus and later Bedrock released the records, and the long-form DJ mixes of the period fixed the idea of the style as a journey heard over a whole set, not a single hook.

Where uplifting trance saves one lead melody for a huge breakdown and drop, progressive trance layers several smaller melodic parts and lets them shift over many bars. Breakdowns are present but subtler, and they often resolve into more tension instead of a release. The bassline follows the chords with a rounder, groovier feel, the kick is deeper, and the payoff arrives by accumulation. Tracks run long, commonly 8-12 minutes in their original mixes.

The style has had two distinct sounds. The 1990s and early-2000s form is dark, hypnotic and close to progressive house. From the 2010s a smoother, more melodic form took over, shaped by labels such as Anjunabeats and Anjunadeep, and in the 2020s it has drifted toward melodic techno. The two share a tempo and a method but not a sound, so naming the one you want matters when prompting.

Progressive trance and progressive house overlap heavily and tempo alone does not separate them. The working difference: progressive trance keeps trance's breakdown-and-build shape and its melodic, emotive harmony, at a slightly higher tempo; progressive house stays flatter, more groove-led and textural. See [Progressive House](../progressive-house/README.md).

## Characteristics

- **Instrumentation**: Deep, rounded four-on-the-floor kick; a bassline that follows the chord tones with groove; plucked synth motifs, arpeggios and evolving pads layered a few at a time; filtered stabs; restrained lead sounds; long delays; in the 1990s form also breakbeat percussion and darker atmospheres

- **Vocals**: Sparse or absent. Typical uses are a breathy phrase, a spoken line or a vocal chop treated as one more layer in the mix; female more often than male; reverb and delay throws. Full sung songs over progressive production belong to [Vocal Trance](../vocal-trance/README.md)

- **Production**: Warm and spacious, with headroom and depth over brightness; slow filter automation across whole sections; elements enter and leave every 8-32 bars; sidechain compression gentler than in uplifting; nothing in the mix shouts

- **Energy/Mood**: Hypnotic, introspective, driving but restrained; melancholic or quietly hopeful; a sense of travel; energy rises by accumulation and falls away slowly

- **Structure**: Long intro for mixing; layers added one at a time; a mid-track breakdown that thins the arrangement without stopping its momentum; a rebuild that returns to the groove with one or two new elements instead of an anthem drop; long outro. No single climax. Original mixes are 8-12 minutes; a shortened edit keeps the gradual build in about 4-5 minutes

- **Tempo**: 124-132 BPM, most often 128-130

## Lyric Conventions

- **Default rhyme scheme**: None or free — a repeated phrase, not a rhymed verse
- **Rhyme quality**: Incidental. Sound matters more than rhyme: soft consonants and long vowels that blend into pads and delays
- **Verse structure**: No verses. One phrase of 1-2 lines, or at most a short 4-line passage, placed in the breakdown and echoed as fragments elsewhere
- **Key rule**: The vocal is a texture inside the arrangement. Write a phrase that can loop without tiring: an image or a feeling, open-ended, with no story to follow. It must never interrupt the groove
- **Avoid**: Verse-chorus structures; big sing-along hooks; narrative or dense lyrics; belted or aggressive delivery; anything that asks for the listener's full attention
- **Density/pacing (Suno)**: Default **0-2 lines/verse** at 124-132 BPM; up to 4 for a breakdown passage. Vocal only in the breakdown, with repeated fragments allowed elsewhere; leave `[Intro]`, `[Build]` and `[Outro]` empty so they are played, not sung. Topics: 1/track. Many tracks are instrumental

## Subgenres & Styles

| Style | Description | Reference Artists |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Classic UK Progressive** | The 1990s sound: dark, hypnotic, long builds and evolving motifs, close to progressive house | Sasha, John Digweed, Way Out West, Tilt |
| **Melodic Progressive (2000s)** | Smoother and more emotive, running alongside mainstream trance; warm pads and gentle leads | Solarstone, Markus Schulz, BT |
| **Anjuna Progressive** | The 2010s label sound: polished, plucked melodies and big but controlled builds at 128-132 BPM | ilan Bluestone, Andrew Bayer, Grum |
| **Deep Progressive** | Slower and more atmospheric, shading into deep and melodic house | Andrew Bayer, Yotto, Tinlicker |
| **Melodic Techno Crossover** | The 2020s direction: darker timbres and techno drums under trance harmony | Mind Against, Cristoph |

## Suno Prompt Keywords

```
progressive trance, 128 BPM, hypnotic trance, deep trance groove,
gradual build, evolving layers, plucked synth motif, warm pads,
arpeggio, deep rounded kick, groovy bassline, four-on-the-floor,
subtle breakdown, filter sweep, long delay, atmospheric,
introspective, melancholic, driving, spacious mix
```

Prompting notes:
- State the tempo as a number, and describe the shape you want (`gradual build`, `subtle breakdown`, `evolving layers`); without it the result drifts toward an anthem structure. Describe what should be there instead of writing "no drop": a negation in the Style Box is not honored.
- Pick the era. For the 1990s form add `dark, hypnotic, underground, breakbeat percussion`; for the modern form add `melodic, polished, plucked lead`. This is the one trance style where an era tag helps.
- Leave out `euphoric`, `anthem`, `supersaw lead` and `epic`: they describe uplifting trance and will turn the track into one.
- To keep it apart from progressive house, keep `trance` in the genre term and mention the breakdown.
- Structure the Lyrics Box as `[Intro]`, `[Build]`, `[Breakdown]`, `[Build]`, `[Outro]`, `[End]`. A full 8-12 minute original mix is longer than a single generation reliably gives; aim for a 4-5 minute edit that still builds slowly.

## Reference Tracks

- **Sasha - "Xpander"** — The form's founding document: eleven minutes of evolving texture with no single hook

- **BT - "Flaming June"** — Melodic progressive at its most lush; shows how emotion is carried by layers instead of one lead

- **Energy 52 - "Café del Mar" (Three 'N One Remix)** — A slow-burning build around one motif; the bridge between progressive and anthem trance

- **Way Out West - "The Gift"** — British progressive with breakbeat percussion and a sampled vocal used as texture

- **Andrew Bayer - "Once Lydian"** — The modern form: detailed sound design and patient development

- **ilan Bluestone - "Spheres"** — 2010s Anjuna progressive; plucked melody and a controlled, groove-first build
